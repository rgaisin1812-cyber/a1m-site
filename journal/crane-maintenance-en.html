Loader crane maintenance: what to inspect and what to tell the service team

Oil around a fitting, a sticking lever and slow boom movement point the mechanic towards different checks. Record where each symptom occurs and the conditions in which it appears.

Hydraulics and moving parts

Inspect hoses for abrasion or cracking and look for oil around connections. Follow the procedure for the particular model when checking the fluid level. The manual also gives the oil specification, lubrication points and service intervals. Looking for a leak by hand on a pressurised system can cause serious injury.

Outriggers, controls and electrics

Look for cracks or deformation in the outriggers. If an outrigger fails to lock, a lever sticks or the load limiter reports a fault, stop operating the machine and tell the service team. Depending on the symptom, a technician may need to inspect the locking mechanism, control linkages, wiring or sensors.

Give the technician a precise description

Record the crane model and the specific function affected. If the boom has slowed down, note which movement is slow and whether it also happens once the oil is warm. A photo of a display fault helps the technician identify the message. When requesting a replacement part, include its catalogue number so the supplier can check the configuration.
